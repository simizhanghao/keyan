\begin{abstract}
Out-of-band (OOB) spectral evidence is attractive for radio-frequency fingerprint identification (RFFI) because transmitter impairments can extend beyond the nominal signal band. The same evidence is also shaped by the receiver front-end, creating a deployment-dependent failure mode that is difficult to diagnose from clean accuracy alone. We present a frozen-checkpoint, intervention-based study of this tension in cross-receiver LoRa RFFI. A signal-level audit first shows that OOB-to-in-band variation contains transmitter-discriminative structure while also exhibiting systematic receiver-associated variation. Two capacity-matched OOB-aware backbones, an in-band control, and strong CIS/receiver-adversarial baselines are then evaluated with deterministic OOB scale, same-receiver cross-device shuffle, neutral replacement, and left/right interventions. On six previously unopened official blind receivers, both OOB-aware backbones reproduce large receiver-level disruption on all six receivers. The median disruption drop is 49.05 percentage points for LateFusion-OOB and 71.78 points for CrossAttn-OOB; the median worst-scale drops are 51.83 and 70.66 points, respectively. Left-side OOB sensitivity exceeds right-side sensitivity on all six receivers for both backbones. In contrast, Shen-style baselines reach 96.41--100.00\% clean accuracy, showing that cross-receiver identification is not intrinsically unsolvable. The results establish a bounded mechanism claim: OOB evidence can be genuinely useful for transmitter discrimination while inducing architecture-dependent, receiver-sensitive and frequency-structured dependencies. Controlled robustness to an isolated OOB perturbation should therefore not be interpreted as real unseen-receiver generalization.
\end{abstract}
