\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{OOB Variation Contains Both Device and Receiver Structure}
The signal-level audit shows that OOB-to-in-band variation is not a receiver-only artifact. The standard deviation of group means is 0.393 dB across devices, 0.111 dB across receiver types, 0.083 dB across receiver instances and 0.015 dB across days (Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}). Thus device structure is the largest observed grouping effect, while receiver-associated variation is substantially larger than the same-receiver day effect. This motivates an intervention study rather than a binary label of OOB as either useful fingerprint or receiver noise.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig2_signal_audit.pdf}
\caption{Signal-level audit of relative OOB magnitude. The grouped standard deviation shows transmitter-discriminative variation together with a systematic receiver component.}
\label{fig:signal}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Frozen Development Interventions Reveal OOB Dependence}
On six pseudo-unseen development receiver folds, scaling OOB bins produces accuracy drops on every fold for both OOB-aware backbones. The median drops are 47.28 points for LateFusion-OOB and 56.60 points for CrossAttn-OOB. Same-receiver cross-device shuffle produces median drops of 47.39 and 60.90 points, respectively, while source-derived neutral replacement produces median drops of 43.94 and 56.77 points. The agreement between shuffle and neutral replacement is important: the response is not explained by a single arbitrary neutral vector. Device probes reach 94.4\% for LateFusion-OOB and 97.9\% for CrossAttn-OOB, whereas receiver probes reach only 16.2\% and 14.7\% against a 7.7\% receiver-group chance level. OOB therefore carries device-discriminative evidence, but the classifier's use of that evidence remains receiver-sensitive.

\caption{Development mechanism diagnosis. Accuracy drops are computed from frozen checkpoints under OOB-only scale and same-receiver cross-device shuffle interventions. Each point is a receiver mean over two seeds.}
\label{fig:development}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Architecture and Frequency Controls Define the Boundary}
Matched development folds show architecture-dependent behavior. LateFusion-OOB reaches 56.64\% while its strict TrueIB control reaches 11.97\%. For the cross-attentive family, the corresponding values are 60.90\% and 70.91\%. Thus an OOB-aware score cannot be interpreted as a universal benefit or penalty independent of representation. The strong Shen-CIS and Shen-RA baselines reach 94.101\% and 93.847\%, respectively, establishing that the receiver-held-out task is solvable under this protocol (Fig.~\ref{fig:controls}).

The left/right intervention provides a frequency-structured diagnostic. On development folds, left-OOB drops are 44.41 and 51.12 points for B1 and C$'$, while right-OOB drops are 17.04 and 1.12 points. We therefore describe the asymmetry as a learned spectral sensitivity, not as proof that a particular analogue component causes receiver shift.

\caption{Matched architecture and strong-baseline controls on development receiver folds. TrueIB is a strict in-band pathway control; Shen-CIS/RA provide strong receiver-held-out baselines.}
\label{fig:controls}

\subsection{Controlled Invariance Does Not Guarantee Real Transfer}
A separate receiver-style augmentation (RSA) reduces sensitivity to the selected controlled OOB-scale nuisance from a 53.96-point development degradation to 2.92 points. However, clean held-out accuracy is 64.26$\pm$14.16\% for the clean continuation (CT) and 60.85$\pm$16.40\% for RSA/F0 (mean$\pm$SD over receiver folds). The result is a negative boundary, not a failed experiment: invariance to one engineered nuisance is not equivalent to generalization across real receiver domains (Fig.~\ref{fig:rsa}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5_mitigation_boundary.pdf}
\caption{Mitigation boundary. (a) Full-scale accuracy drop (percentage points) for the clean continuation (CT) and RSA/F0. (b) Clean held-out accuracy (mean$\pm$SD across receiver folds). Suppressing the selected scale response does not imply improved receiver-held-out accuracy.}
\label{fig:rsa}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Blind Confirmation on Previously Unopened Receivers}
The protocol-frozen confirmatory blind evaluation reproduces the mechanism on every official blind receiver. For LateFusion-OOB, the receiver-level median disruption drop is 49.05 points and the median worst-scale drop is 51.83 points. For CrossAttn-OOB, the corresponding values are 71.78 and 70.66 points. All six receivers are positive for both effects in both architectures. Left-minus-right drop is positive on all six receivers, with receiver medians of 29.89 and 70.56 points for LateFusion-OOB and CrossAttn-OOB, respectively. Both backbones satisfied all prespecified confirmatory criteria (Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}).

\centering\includegraphics[width=.93\textwidth]{fig6_blind_confirmation.pdf}
\caption{Protocol-frozen confirmatory blind evaluation across six receiver domains. All six domains reproduce the reported OOB utility, scale sensitivity and left/right asymmetry.}
