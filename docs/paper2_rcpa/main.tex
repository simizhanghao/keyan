\begin{abstract}
Low-power LoRa networks enable large-scale IoT connectivity, but gateway-side radio frequency fingerprint identification (RFFI) often degrades when the receiving front-end changes.
Transmitter hardware impairments are observed through receiver-specific spectral response, gain, and filtering, so models trained on one receiver may fail on another even when the transmitter population is unchanged.
Out-of-band (OOB) evidence can strengthen same-receiver LoRa authentication when fused with in-band representations, yet our diagnosis shows that OOB features become receiver-entangled under cross-receiver shift.
We analyze this failure at physical, representation, and decision layers through OOB spectral profiling, linear receiver probes, embedding-distance ratios, and prediction-collapse analysis on a frozen OOB-guided hybrid backbone.
We then introduce Receiver-Calibrated Prototype Adaptation (RCPA), a lightweight post-hoc method that rebuilds target-receiver class prototypes from $K$ labeled calibration windows per device under a block-disjoint protocol.
Across RX1$\leftrightarrow$RX2 transfer, three checkpoint seeds, and three split repeats, RCPA-T improves pooled file-level accuracy from approximately 20\% (source classifier) to 58.3\% at $K{=}5$, 69.4\% at $K{=}10$, and 75.0\% at $K{=}20$, while unsupervised test-time adaptation and source-only prototypes remain near chance.
OOB representation equalization suppresses receiver probe accuracy but does not replace labeled calibration.
These results support a diagnosis-first cross-receiver RFFI framework rather than a new backbone architecture.
\end{abstract}
