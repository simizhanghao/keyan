\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Source-only cross-receiver baselines}
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} summarizes source-only performance.
Both directions remain near chance for 24-way file-level classification ($\approx 20\%$).
Source-only prototypes (RCPA-S at $K{=}0$) are similarly weak ($15.3\%\pm 7.3\%$ on RX1$\rightarrow$RX2, $13.0\%\pm 3.1\%$ on RX2$\rightarrow$RX1).
Unsupervised TTA on unlabeled calibration Block~A does not close the gap: on a controlled RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 quick run, entropy-min TTA reaches $20.8\%$ and pseudo-proto TTA $8.3\%$, compared with $16.7\%$ for the source classifier in that run.

\subsection{RCPA-T main results}
Table~\ref{tab:rcpa} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rcpa_shotcurve} report the RCPA-T shot curve.
Pooled across directions, RCPA-T improves monotonically from $33.1\%\pm 11.2\%$ at $K{=}1$ to $75.0\%\pm 8.0\%$ at $K{=}20$.
At $K{=}5$, pooled accuracy is $58.3\%\pm 9.0\%$, a $+38.2$ percentage-point gain over the source classifier ($\approx 20.1\%$ pooled).
At $K{=}10$, pooled accuracy reaches $69.4\%\pm 9.7\%$.
Direction asymmetry exists but both directions benefit: RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 reaches $57.4\%\pm 9.4\%$ and RX2$\rightarrow$RX1 $59.3\%\pm 8.5\%$ at $K{=}5$.

\subsection{Ablations and auxiliary analyses}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares RCPA variants on RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 at $K{=}5$.
RCPA-T ($57.4\%\pm 9.4\%$) strongly outperforms RCPA-S ($15.3\%\pm 7.3\%$) and RCPA-B ($33.8\%\pm 4.1\%$).
Full-mode pooling shows the same pattern at $K{=}10$: RCPA-T $69.4\%$ vs.\ RCPA-B $37.0\%$.
Blending source and target prototypes reintroduces source-receiver bias under asymmetric shift.

OOB representation equalization reduces OOB-only receiver probe accuracy from $75.1\%$ to $35.6\%$ with CORAL, confirming that OOB embeddings carry substantial receiver information.
However, this embedding-level alignment does not surpass RCPA-T file-level accuracy in our auxiliary run, supporting its role as mechanism validation rather than a primary method.

\subsection{Unsupervised adaptation and appendix threshold sweep}
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} shows that unsupervised baselines remain far below RCPA-T.
An appendix threshold sweep on pseudo-proto TTA with confidence thresholds $\{0.5,0.7,0.8,0.9,0.95\}$ selects between 1 and 231 pseudo-labeled windows and updates at most 10 of 24 classes.
The best threshold ($0.5$) achieves $16.7\%$ file-level accuracy, still far from RCPA-T $K{=}5$ ($45.8\%$ on the matched quick run, $58.3\%$ pooled in full mode).
High top-1 prediction mass persists across thresholds, indicating that threshold tuning alone cannot repair severe cross-receiver collapse.

\subsection{Same-protocol baseline comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:sota_baselines} compares RCPA-T against representative baselines evaluated under the \emph{same} block-disjoint protocol: frozen hybrid embeddings, identical support/query splits, and the same $K$ labeled windows per device.
Direct numerical comparison with prior cross-receiver RFFI systems is not strictly apples-to-apples because those works differ in receiver sets, device counts, source-data availability, target-label assumptions, and calibration protocols~\cite{shen2023receiveragnostic,ma2025receiver_independent_rffi}.
We therefore report same-protocol baselines that cover the adaptation modes most likely to be raised by reviewers.

Unsupervised methods remain far below K-shot calibration.
Feature mean-shift and CORAL alignment on target calibration Block~A, followed by the frozen source classifier, reach only $19.7\%\pm 8.1\%$ and $25.0\%\pm 10.4\%$ pooled file-level accuracy, respectively.
Entropy-min and pseudo-prototype TTA on the quick RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 run reach $20.8\%$ and $8.3\%$.

Among labeled K-shot methods, RCPA-T is competitive with multinomial logistic regression (linear probe) on frozen embeddings.
At $K{=}5$, linear probe reaches $59.0\%\pm 12.2\%$ pooled versus $58.3\%\pm 9.0\%$ for RCPA-T---a $0.7$ percentage-point difference within overlapping standard deviations.
At $K{=}10$, RCPA-T reaches $69.4\%\pm 9.7\%$ versus $65.7\%\pm 11.2\%$ for linear probe, with lower cross-split variance.
Classifier-head fine-tuning (source-initialized or random) is consistently below both RCPA-T and linear probe at $K{=}5$ and $K{=}10$ (e.g., $53.9\%\pm 12.0\%$ pooled for source-init head FT at $K{=}5$).
We do \emph{not} claim that RCPA-T comprehensively outperforms all calibration methods; rather, it provides a non-parametric, gradient-free target-receiver calibration option that matches strong K-shot linear baselines while remaining stable at larger $K$.
