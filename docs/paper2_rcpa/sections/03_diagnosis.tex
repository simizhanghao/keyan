\section{Cross-Receiver Failure Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
We consider closed-set LoRa device identification under cross-receiver transfer~\cite{elmaghbub2021lora_wild,yang2024cross_receiver_da,yang2025scrffi}.
Let $\mathcal{D}_s=\{(x_i,y_i)\}$ denote labeled captures collected at source receiver $r_s$ for devices $y_i\in\{1,\ldots,C\}$.
At deployment, traffic is observed at target receiver $r_t\neq r_s$.
The model must classify each target-receiver device file correctly after minimal target-side calibration.

We use a frozen hybrid RF feature extractor $f(\cdot)$ trained on $r_s$ from our related submitted OOB-guided RF-HSTU work~\cite{paper1_placeholder}.
For each window $w$, path-specific embeddings are fused into $z(w)\in\mathbb{R}^d$.
All diagnosis below is performed on extracted embeddings and OOB spectra; we do not retrain the backbone.
Prior cross-receiver studies show that receiver front-end impairments interfere with transmitter fingerprint extraction~\cite{shen2024receiveragnostic,ma2025receiver_independent_rffi,alshawabka2020exposing_fingerprint}, but they do not analyze OOB-path entanglement under a frozen hybrid backbone.

\subsection{Receiver-induced feature entanglement}
Cross-receiver failure is not explained by complete embedding collapse alone~\cite{cekic2020robust_wireless_fingerprinting}.
File-level cosine analysis shows that same-device cross-receiver pairs can remain relatively close for the hybrid fused embedding, while device separability and decision boundaries degrade.
We therefore analyze receiver entanglement at three levels:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Physical layer:} OOB spectral energy bias between receivers.
  \item \textbf{Representation layer:} linear receiver/device probes on main, OOB, and fused paths.
  \item \textbf{Decision layer:} file-level accuracy, confusion structure, and top-1 prediction mass.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{OOB path receiver probe}
We train linear receiver probes on file-level mean embeddings pooled across RX1 and RX2.
For the hybrid model, the OOB-only path yields the highest receiver-probe accuracy ($72.7\%$), compared with main ($62.4\%$) and fused ($50.2\%$).
The OOB path also yields higher device-probe accuracy on mixed-receiver windows ($28.9\%$) than the main path ($5.7\%$), indicating that OOB carries both device and receiver information simultaneously.
Prior OOB studies establish that spectral leakage can encode hardware evidence~\cite{elmaghbub2020widescan,hamdaoui2022deeplearning}, but under cross-receiver shift the receiver component can dominate transfer failure.

\subsection{OOB spectral receiver bias}
Physical-layer OOB analysis compares mean OOB energy spectra between RX2 and RX1.
The aggregate RX2/RX1 OOB energy ratio is $1.44$, with per-device mean shift $0.76$ and ratio-spectrum standard deviation $0.17$.
This supports systematic receiver front-end spectral bias rather than transmitter-only variation and provides a physical explanation for elevated OOB receiver probe accuracy.

\subsection{Embedding distance ratio}
Let $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ denote cosine distance between file-level mean embeddings.
We compare same-device cross-receiver distance against different-device same-receiver distance and report their ratio.
CNN-IQ exhibits ratio $1.25>1$, indicating that receiver shift dominates device separation in the CNN embedding space.
The hybrid fused embedding shows ratio $0.22<1$, meaning same-device cross-receiver pairs remain directionally closer than different-device pairs on the same receiver.
However, mixed-receiver device linear probe accuracy remains only $28.9\%$ for 24 classes, so partial geometric preservation does not imply transferable decision boundaries.

\subsection{Confusion and prediction collapse}
Under RX1$\rightarrow$RX2, CNN-IQ collapses to one or two dominant predicted classes with top-1 prediction mass up to $95.8\%$ at file level.
The hybrid model performs better but remains near chance for 24-way file-level classification ($\approx 12$--$21\%$ across seeds).
RX2$\rightarrow$RX1 is asymmetric, confirming that the two receivers form non-symmetric domains rather than a simple sign flip~\cite{yang2024cross_receiver_da}.

\subsection{Diagnosis conclusion}
Cross-receiver failure arises from receiver-induced OOB entanglement and non-transferable source decision geometry.
The frozen hybrid backbone retains partial same-device structure, making lightweight target-receiver prototype calibration a plausible repair mechanism rather than full backbone retraining or multi-receiver adversarial retraining~\cite{shen2024receiveragnostic,ma2025cross_receiver_disentangle}.
