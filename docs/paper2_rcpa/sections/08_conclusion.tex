\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied cross-receiver LoRa RFFI failure through an OOB-aware diagnosis pipeline and proposed RCPA, a lightweight post-hoc target-receiver prototype calibration method built on a frozen hybrid backbone.
Our analysis shows that OOB features can become receiver-entangled under cross-receiver shift, that source classifier and source prototype decisions are not transferable, and that RCPA-T restores device separability using only $K$ labeled calibration windows per device under a block-disjoint protocol.
Across two transfer directions, three checkpoint seeds, and three split repeats, RCPA-T improves pooled file-level accuracy from approximately 20\% to 58.3\% at $K{=}5$, 69.4\% at $K{=}10$, and 75.0\% at $K{=}20$.
Unsupervised target adaptation remains insufficient under severe collapse, while OOB representation equalization suppresses receiver probe without replacing labeled calibration.
Future work may explore waveform-level OOB equalization, broader receiver populations, and target-day calibration under separate evaluation protocols.
