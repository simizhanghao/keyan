\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{RF Fingerprinting and LoRa RFFI}
Radio frequency fingerprint identification exploits transmitter-specific hardware impairments---power-amplifier nonlinearity, I/Q imbalance, and filter imperfections---for device verification~\cite{brik2008wireless_device_id,jian2020deeplearning_rf_fingerprinting,riyaz2018deep_cnn_radio_id}.
Large-scale studies and ORACLE-style CNN classifiers demonstrate that deep models can learn discriminative RF patterns from IQ snapshots~\cite{sankhe2019oracle,jian2020deeplearning_rf_fingerprinting}.
Surveys summarize physical-layer authentication, device fingerprinting challenges, and IoT-oriented RFFI datasets~\cite{xie2021physical_layer_auth_survey,xu2015device_fingerprinting_survey,xie2024rffi_iot_survey,zhang2019phy_layer_security_iot}.
LoRa RFFI is complicated by chirp spread spectrum modulation and by sensitivity to collection conditions~\cite{shen2021lora_jsac,shen2021lora_spectrogram_cnn,alshawabka2021deeplora}.
The OSU LoRa corpus and follow-on studies systematically expose cross-day, cross-location, cross-configuration, and cross-receiver degradation~\cite{elmaghbub2021lora_wild,hamdaoui2022deeplearning,elmaghbub2021dataset_release,ahmed2024lora_rffi_survey}.
Our work adopts the cross-receiver subset of that corpus and evaluates under strict block-disjoint calibration/query splits rather than random window mixing.

\subsection{OOB Evidence in LoRa RFFI}
WideScan shows that out-of-band distortion caused by power-amplifier nonlinearity and filter roll-off can strengthen device signatures beyond in-band views~\cite{elmaghbub2020widescan}.
Follow-on LoRa studies further analyze deployment variability and deep feature designs for RF-data-driven classification~\cite{hamdaoui2022deeplearning,hamdaoui2020deep_nn_features,elmaghbub2021lora_wild}.
IoTJ and related work emphasize robust fingerprint extraction under variable channel and hardware conditions~\cite{zhou2021robust_rffi_iotj,xie2018coherent_rff_iotj}.
We do not claim the first use of OOB evidence.
Instead, we study what happens to OOB-guided representations when the receiving front-end changes: OOB can become receiver-entangled rather than purely device-discriminative, which motivates diagnosis before calibration.

\subsection{Cross-Receiver and Receiver-Agnostic RFFI}
Receiver mismatch is a known open challenge in LoRa deployment studies~\cite{elmaghbub2021lora_wild,alshawabka2020exposing_fingerprint}.
\textbf{Multi-receiver retraining and adversarial objectives.}
Receiver-agnostic RFFI learns receiver-independent features through adversarial training and collaborative multi-receiver inference, often requiring coordinated multi-receiver data collection~\cite{shen2024receiveragnostic,ganin2016domain_adversarial,ma2025receiver_independent_rffi}.
Recent disentanglement-based cross-receiver generalization further separates transmitter and receiver factors during training~\cite{ma2025cross_receiver_disentangle}.
\textbf{Unlabeled target-receiver adaptation.}
Cross-receiver domain adaptation and source-free SCRFFI adapt a source-trained model to a new receiver using unlabeled target traffic, pseudo labels, or contrastive objectives~\cite{yang2024cross_receiver_da,yang2025scrffi,liang2020shot}.
\textbf{Channel-robust metric learning.}
Scalable and contrastive LoRa RFFI methods improve embedding compactness under channel variation but typically retrain the backbone under different enrollment assumptions~\cite{shen2022scalable_lora_rffi,ma2025contrastive_lora_rffi,khosla2020supcon,wen2016center_loss}.
\textbf{Our frozen-backbone K-window calibration.}
We differ from the above directions by freezing a same-receiver backbone, diagnosing OOB entanglement on target-receiver data, and applying post-hoc prototype or linear calibration with minimal labeled target windows.
\textbf{Direct numerical comparison with prior cross-receiver RFFI systems is not apples-to-apples} because they differ in receiver sets, devices, source-data access, target-label availability, and calibration protocols.
Our experiments instead compare against representative \emph{same-protocol} baselines under identical block-disjoint splits (Tables~\ref{tab:unlabeled_baselines} and~\ref{tab:kshot_baselines}).

\subsection{Source-Free and Unsupervised Adaptation}
Test-time adaptation methods such as TENT update normalization or lightweight adapter statistics on unlabeled target data by minimizing prediction entropy~\cite{wang2021tent,lim2023ttn}.
Source-free domain adaptation frameworks such as SHOT transfer a source hypothesis without accessing source data~\cite{liang2020shot,ben2010domain_adaptation}.
In cross-receiver RFFI, SCRFFI and related methods target adaptation with target-unlabeled data only~\cite{yang2025scrffi,yang2024cross_receiver_da}.
These approaches assume a different deployment mode from ours: we allow a small set of labeled target-receiver calibration windows on a frozen backbone.
Our TTA diagnostic baselines, threshold sweep, and CORAL/mean-shift feature-alignment baselines show that unsupervised repair remains near chance under severe RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 collapse, supporting labeled calibration as the more reliable option under our protocol~\cite{sun2016deepcoral,wang2021tent}.

\subsection{Few-Shot and Prototype Classification}
Prototypical classification represents each class by the mean embedding of a support set and assigns queries by nearest-prototype distance~\cite{snell2017prototypical}.
Metric-learning and center-loss objectives are widely used to improve class compactness in RFFI embeddings~\cite{wen2016center_loss,khosla2020supcon,shen2022scalable_lora_rffi}.
Few-shot and open-set RFFI studies often use episodic meta-learning or multiple enrollment captures per device~\cite{cekic2020robust_wireless_fingerprinting}.
We do not claim a new prototype algorithm.
Instead, we compare RCPA-T against K-shot logistic regression and classifier-head fine-tuning under a LoRa-specific protocol with one file per device per receiver, where $K$ denotes labeled windows and calibration/support/query blocks are explicitly disjoint.
Same-protocol results (Tables~\ref{tab:unlabeled_baselines} and~\ref{tab:kshot_baselines}) show that RCPA-T is competitive with linear probe while avoiding gradient-based target adaptation.
