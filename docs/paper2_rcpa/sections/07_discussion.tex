\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why RCPA-T works}
Target-receiver prototypes re-estimate class geometry in the receiver-observed embedding space without modifying the backbone.
The diagnosis shows that source decision boundaries are misaligned even when same-device cross-receiver embeddings remain partially close.
RCPA-T therefore repairs the \emph{decision layer} using minimal labeled target evidence rather than attempting to learn receiver-invariant features from source data alone.

\subsection{Why RCPA-B underperforms}
Blending source and target prototypes can reintroduce source-receiver bias under asymmetric shift.
Our ablation shows RCPA-B consistently below RCPA-T at $K{=}5$ and $K{=}10$, suggesting that target-only prototypes are preferable when the goal is to match the target receiver's observed impairment geometry.

\subsection{Why unsupervised adaptation fails}
Entropy-min and pseudo-proto TTA rely on confident, class-diverse target predictions to provide useful self-supervision.
Under RX1$\rightarrow$RX2 collapse, prediction mass concentrates on a small subset of classes and pseudo-label coverage spans at most ten classes even at low thresholds.
This matches the diagnosis: when receiver entanglement dominates, unlabeled adaptation cannot reliably infer device identity on the target receiver.
Same-protocol feature alignment (mean-shift, CORAL) improves over chance only marginally and remains far below K-shot labeled calibration (Table~\ref{tab:sota_baselines}).

\subsection{RCPA-T vs.\ K-shot parametric calibration}
Table~\ref{tab:sota_baselines} shows that RCPA-T is not the only viable repair under labeled target windows.
A K-shot linear probe on frozen embeddings performs similarly at $K{=}5$ and slightly lower at $K{=}10$.
We therefore frame RCPA-T as a \emph{lightweight non-parametric} calibration choice rather than a claim of universal superiority over all adaptation methods.
Its practical advantages are: (i) no gradient-based target optimization or learning-rate tuning; (ii) stable performance across split repeats at $K{=}10$ (pooled std $9.7\%$ vs.\ $11.2\%$ for linear probe); and (iii) consistent gains over source-target prototype blending (RCPA-B) and head fine-tuning under the same protocol.
When optimization is acceptable, K-shot linear calibration can be adopted as a stronger parametric alternative; RCPA-T remains the default when deployment simplicity and non-parametric stability are prioritized.

\subsection{Relation to prior/submitted backbone work}
This paper is complementary to prior OOB-guided RF-HSTU modeling~\cite{paper1_placeholder}.
That work addresses same-receiver robustness, architecture design, and source-only cross-day evaluation; it reports strict cross-receiver transfer as an open limitation.
The present work uses the same backbone family as a frozen extractor but asks a different question: \emph{why} cross-receiver transfer fails and \emph{how} to recover separability with lightweight target calibration.
We do not claim improved same-receiver cross-day accuracy and do not propose a new backbone.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single indoor cross-receiver setup.} Results are from one controlled Diff\_Receivers indoor collection; outdoor, multi-gateway, and multi-vendor receivers remain to be tested.
  \item \textbf{One file per device per receiver.} $K$ is measured in windows, not independent enrollment files; window-level support is necessary because of the dataset structure and must be stated clearly in deployment discussions.
  \item \textbf{Labeled target windows required for RCPA-T.} This is not source-only cross-receiver authentication; it is a calibration-aware deployment mode suitable when a small labeled enrollment pass is feasible at the target gateway.
  \item \textbf{OOB-Eq is embedding-level.} CORAL on OOB embeddings validates mechanism but is not waveform-level spectral equalization.
  \item \textbf{TTA baselines are single-direction quick runs plus appendix sweep.} Full-direction TTA sweeps may be added later but are unlikely to change the conclusion given current collapse evidence.
  \item \textbf{No cross-day RCPA evaluation.} Target-day calibration is a different shift type from target-receiver calibration and is intentionally left for future companion analysis to avoid mixing narratives with Paper~1 cross-day claims.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deployment interpretation}
Three deployment modes emerge from our study.
When no target labels exist, source-only operation remains the honest baseline and should be expected to perform near chance under severe receiver mismatch.
When unlabeled target traffic is abundant but labels are unavailable, unsupervised adaptation may help under mild shift but is unreliable under the collapse regime documented here.
When a small labeled calibration pass is feasible, RCPA-T offers a practical middle ground: no backbone retraining, block-disjoint evaluation, and substantial accuracy recovery with modest $K$.

\subsection{Venue and narrative fit}
The diagnosis-first story fits journals emphasizing IoT security and physical-layer authentication (IoTJ, TIFS) because it connects hardware-level OOB bias to gateway deployment risk.
The calibration protocol and negative TTA results also align with wireless ML and domain-shift venues (TWC, Sensors) if the narrative emphasizes protocol honesty and failure analysis rather than architecture novelty.
