\title{OOB-Guided Cross-Attentive RF-HSTU Hybrid Modeling for Robust LoRa Device Authentication}

\author{Author~One, Author~Two%
\thanks{Manuscript prepared on branch \texttt{paper-ready-v3}. Affiliations TBD.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Low-power wide-area IoT networks based on LoRa face cloning, spoofing, and long-term deployment drift.
Radio frequency fingerprint identification (RFFI) offers a lightweight physical-layer complement to cryptographic authentication at edge gateways.
However, IQ-only convolutional baselines often fail to generalize under cross-day channel variation.
We propose an out-of-band (OOB) guided hybrid architecture combining a CNN stem for local RF impairments, an RF-HSTU sequence encoder for LoRa chirp-structured patch tokens, chirp positional embeddings, and OOB-guided cross-attention for adaptive hardware-distortion evidence injection.
On the public OSU LoRa dataset with 24 devices, under strict source-domain validation (Day1--3 train, Day4 validation, Day5 test), our hybrid model achieves \textbf{75.0$\pm$5.3\%} file-level accuracy versus \textbf{54.2$\pm$14.2\%} for a reproduced CNN-IQ baseline, with a pooled bootstrap 95\% confidence interval of \textbf{[+9.2, +32.5]} percentage points on the paired gain.
Controlled fusion ablations show that cross-attentive OOB fusion---rather than chirp embedding or naive concatenation---drives stable cross-day gains.
Extended deployment-shift experiments indicate promising robustness under distance and indoor-location shifts, while configuration, outdoor-location, and strict source-only cross-receiver transfer remain challenging.
\end{abstract}
